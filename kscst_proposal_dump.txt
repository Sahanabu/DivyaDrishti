  Row 1:  | Background and Need of the Project:  1. Background: Road accidents caused by rider fatigue, delayed reactions, unsafe driving conditions, and unexpected road hazards remain significant road-safety concerns. DivyaDrishti is an AI- and IoT-based smart helmet system designed to improve rider safety through real-time monitoring, intelligent hazard detection, and emergency assistance. 2. Existing Scenario: Conventional helmets primarily provide physical head protection but generally lack intelligent monitoring and emergency communication capabilities. Some existing smart helmets offer individual features such as GPS tracking, alcohol detection, or crash alerts. However, integrating rider condition monitoring, road-object detection, accident detection, location tracking, and automated emergency notifications into a unified, cost-effective system remains a practical challenge. 3. Problem Statement: Motorcycle riders may experience drowsiness, encounter hazardous road conditions, or become involved in accidents without receiving timely warnings or obtaining immediate assistance. The absence of an integrated, real-time safety system can delay accident identification, location sharing, and emergency response. 4. Need for the Project: DivyaDrishti aims to develop an intelligent safety system integrating AI-based drowsiness detection, computer-vision-based road-object detection, sensor-assisted crash detection, GPS location tracking, and IoT-enabled emergency alerts. Machine-learning techniques will analyse relevant sensor and vision data to assess potential risks and support timely warnings. The system will also facilitate location sharing and notifications to designated family members during emergencies, helping improve rider awareness and response time. 5. Beneficiaries: The primary beneficiaries are motorcycle riders, particularly daily commuters, students, delivery personnel, and long-distance travellers. Family members, emergency responders, traffic-safety authorities, and public transportation-safety initiatives may also benefit from faster accident notification, improved location accessibility, and enhanced road-safety awareness.

Its key innovation is the coordinated use of rider-condition data and road-environment information to support timely safety warnings and emergency response. HOW IS IT DIFFERENT FROM EXISTING WORK? Many existing smart helmet solutions focus on individual functions, such as accident detection, location tracking, alcohol detection, or emergency messaging. DivyaDrishti seeks to integrate multiple safety functions into a unified platform. It combines camera-based road-scene analysis and drowsiness detection with sensor-based crash identification, allowing rider condition and surrounding hazards to be monitored within the same system. Its proposed emergency-response mechanism will transmit GPS location information and notifications to designated family members when a suspected accident is detected. Unlike a system that relies only on accident alerts and location sharing, DivyaDrishti additionally aims to provide a short incident video clip for contextual information and a QR-accessible emergency portal for quick access to rider details and emergency contacts. The video-sharing feature supports digital incident evidence, while the QR portal provides an additional access method that does not depend on the helmet's electronic components being operational. Its modular architecture is intended to support testing, upgrades, and cost-conscious implementation. 3. WHY IS IT INNOVATIVE? The innovation lies in the practical integration of AI-based perception, IoT sensing, and emergency communication into a single rider-safety solution. Rather than relying exclusively on a crash event to initiate assistance, DivyaDrishti aims to identify certain risks before an incident and respond to suspected accidents afterwards. The prototype will be evaluated for detection performance, response time, false alerts, notification reliability, power consumption, and cost under controlled test conditions. The integration of automated incident-video sharing and a QR-based emergency information portal strengthens the emergency-response approach by combining real-time alerts, location information, visual incident context, and readily accessible rider details. The QR code can remain accessible even if the electronic system loses power, provided the printed code and associated online portal remain available. This approach could contribute to the development of accessible, intelligent safety systems for everyday motorcycle riders.
  Row 6:  | Proposed Methodology: 1. OVERALL METHODOLOGY The proposed DivyaDrishti system follows a modular, four-layer architecture integrating embedded hardware, artificial intelligence, backend services, and a real-time monitoring dashboard. Initially, an ESP32 microcontroller will collect sensor data from the MPU6050 inertial measurement unit (IMU), L86-M33 GPS module, MQ-3 alcohol sensor, and MFRC522 RFID reader. Rider information and safety status will be displayed on an SH1106 OLED, with a buzzer providing local alerts. The ESP32 will transmit sensor telemetry to the backend through Wi-Fi. Simultaneously, a camera connected to a laptop will supply video frames to a Python-based AI pipeline. YOLOv8n will detect road objects, computer vision techniques using HLS colour analysis and Sobel edge detection will identify lane boundaries and potential lane deviation, and MediaPipe facial landmarks with Eye Aspect Ratio (EAR) analysis will support drowsiness detection. The extracted visual features will be combined with hardware telemetry to form a 12-dimensional feature vector. A Random Forest classifier will analyse these features to classify driving events and estimate risk levels. Predictions will be transmitted to the Node.js backend, which stores system data in MongoDB Atlas and streams live updates to a React dashboard. When an emergency condition is detected, the system will initiate a cancellable SOS countdown and send GPS-linked alerts through SMS and push notifications. 2. BLOCK DIAGRAM / FLOW CHART  3. SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE  The system consists of four layers: Layer 1 – Hardware and Embedded System: An ESP32-based helmet unit interfaces with the MPU6050, L86-M33 GPS, MQ-3 alcohol sensor, MFRC522 RFID reader, SH1106 OLED, and piezoelectric buzzer. Arduino C++ firmware collects sensor readings, manages rider authentication, detects threshold-based impact events, displays system status, and transmits JSON telemetry through HTTP requests over Wi-Fi. Layer 2 – AI and Computer Vision: A Python-based edge-computing pipeline runs on a laptop and processes camera frames. YOLOv8n identifies safety-relevant road objects, HLS and Sobel-based polynomial lane detection analyses lane boundaries, and MediaPipe calculates eye-related features for drowsiness monitoring. Sensor and vision features are combined and analysed by a Random Forest classifier. FastAPI provides the video-streaming and incident-clip export interfaces. Layer 3 – Backend and Database: Node.js and Express manage incoming sensor telemetry, AI predictions, authentication sessions, and emergency events. Socket.IO broadcasts real-time updates, while MongoDB Atlas stores telemetry readings, predictions, session information, and emergency logs. Layer 4 – Dashboard and Emergency Response: A React-based dashboard displays GPS location using Leaflet, historical telemetry using Recharts, live camera footage, rider status, and AI predictions. The emergency module provides a cancellable SOS countdown and integrates Twilio SMS and NTFY.SH push notifications with GPS coordinates and incident-video links. 4. DESIGN APPROACH The design will follow a modular, incremental implementation approach. Hardware development: Interface the sensors with the ESP32, implement non-blocking firmware tasks, and verify sensor readings, GPS coordinates, RFID authentication, OLED display operation, and buzzer alerts. AI module development: Implement the YOLOv8n object detector, lane detection using HLS colour space and Sobel gradients with polynomial fitting, and MediaPipe-based drowsiness detection. Machine-learning integration: Construct a 12-dimensional feature vector from visual features and hardware telemetry. Train and validate Random Forest models for driving-event classification and risk stratification using the synthetic dataset described in the project report. Backend and dashboard development: Integrate ESP32 telemetry and AI predictions through REST APIs, persist the data in MongoDB Atlas, and display live system information through the React dashboard using Socket.IO. Emergency-response integration: Configure crash and prolonged eye-closure event handling, a 10-second cancellable SOS countdown, incident-video export, and SMS and push notifications containing GPS location information. System integration: Combine the hardware, AI, backend, and dashboard modules and verify end-to-end operation before performing controlled scenario-based testing. 5. EXPERIMENTAL PLAN The prototype will be evaluated through component-level, software-level, integration, and controlled scenario-based testing. Hardware testing: Verify MPU6050 sensor communication and motion readings, GPS coordinate acquisition, MQ-3 response, RFID card identification, OLED rendering, and buzzer operation. Object-detection testing: Evaluate YOLOv8n using labelled images and video clips containing vehicles, motorcycles, pedestrians, and other supported object classes. Measure precision, recall, and detection performance. Lane-detection testing: Use recorded road videos or a controlled test lane with visible markings. Evaluate lane identification, deviation detection, and false warnings under different lighting and marking conditions. Drowsiness testing: Test the MediaPipe-based module using eyes-open, eyes-closed, and blinking scenarios. Measure classification performance and detection response time. Machine-learning evaluation: Train the Random Forest models using the report's synthetic driving dataset and evaluate them using a held-out test set. Record accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, and confusion matrices. Synthetic-data performance will be reported separately from real-world performance. Integration testing: Verify communication between the ESP32, AI pipeline, backend, MongoDB Atlas, and React dashboard. Check telemetry updates, prediction delivery, GPS-map updates, and emergency-event processing. Emergency-response testing: Simulate crash events and prolonged eye closure in controlled conditions. Measure false alerts, SOS countdown operation, GPS-link availability, notification-delivery success, incident-video export, and end-to-end response time. Performance assessment: Measure video-processing frame rate, processing latency, notification latency, power consumption, connectivity reliability, and overall prototype cost. Testing will initially use recorded data, simulation, and controlled stationary or closed-area scenarios. Actual accidents will not be induced, and unsafe public-road testing will be avoided. 6. SOFTWARE AND HARDWARE TO BE USED Hardware ESP32 WROOM-32 development board – embedded processing and Wi-Fi communication. MPU6050 six-axis IMU – acceleration, angular motion, and impact-related measurements. Quectel L86-M33 GPS module – geographical location tracking. MQ-3 alcohol sensor – alcohol-related sensor readings. MFRC522 RFID reader and compatible cards – rider authentication. SH1106 1.3-inch OLED display – rider and system-status information. Piezoelectric buzzer – audible safety warnings. USB webcam or compatible camera – real-time video acquisition. Laptop with a suitable processor, memory, and camera interface – AI edge processing and application hosting. Regulated power supply, connecting wires, helmet mounting arrangements, and supporting electronic components. Software, Frameworks, and Libraries Embedded firmware: Arduino IDE, ESP32 Arduino Core, Arduino C++, ArduinoJson, TinyGPSPlus, MFRC522, and Adafruit SH110X. Computer vision and AI: Python, OpenCV, Ultralytics YOLOv8n, MediaPipe, NumPy, and scikit-learn. AI server: FastAPI and Uvicorn for API services and video streaming. Backend: Node.js, Express.js, and Socket.IO. Database: MongoDB Atlas for telemetry, predictions, session records, and emergency logs. Frontend: React.js, Leaflet, React-Leaflet, Recharts, and Tailwind CSS. Emergency communication: Twilio SMS, NTFY.SH push notifications, GPS-based map links, and incident-video export. Communication protocols: I²C for compatible sensors, UART for GPS, SPI for RFID, HTTP/REST for telemetry and predictions, and Socket.IO for real-time dashboard updates. The system will be developed and evaluated using a combination of embedded hardware tests, AI performance measurements, integration tests, and controlled simulations to assess feasibility, reliability, and suitability for further improvement.
